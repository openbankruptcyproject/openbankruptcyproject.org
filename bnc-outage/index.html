The Bankruptcy Noticing Center outage: what it means for you

Since early October 2026, the system that mails bankruptcy court notices nationwide has been down after a cybersecurity incident at its contractor. The courts are open. The mail is not moving the way it normally does. Below are the courts' own statements, dated and linked, and the practical difference for a debtor, a creditor, or a lawyer.

Published October 22, 2026. Last checked against the sources below on October 22, 2026. Not legal advice.
Status as of October 22, 2026: the Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts reported on October 15 that a cybersecurity incident at BAE Systems, the contractor that operates the Bankruptcy Noticing Center (BNC), has made the BNC's systems and websites unavailable and halted the BNC's production and distribution of bankruptcy notices. No restoration date was stated. Source: uscourts.gov, October 15, 2026. This paragraph is updated by hand. Your court's own website is the authority for your case.

What the BNC is, and what stopped

The Bankruptcy Noticing Center is the service the federal bankruptcy courts use to send notices to people who are not registered for electronic filing. When a court issues the notice of a meeting of creditors, an order, or a hearing notice, the BNC prints and mails it, or delivers it electronically to creditors who signed up for that. It is run by a contractor on the courts' behalf. Its websites, including the National Creditor Registration Service, went offline in the incident.

The courts themselves did not stop. Electronic filing through CM/ECF continues. Registered users, which means most attorneys, trustees, and institutional creditors, still receive Notices of Electronic Filing by e-mail. Dockets are current. Hearings are being held.

What it changes, by who you are

If you are a debtor

Your case is still moving. Deadlines in your case have not been paused by the outage, and your hearing dates stand unless your court says otherwise. If you expected a mailed notice and it has not come, check the docket through your attorney, through PACER, or by calling the clerk's office. If you filed something yourself that the rules require you to serve, that duty was always yours and the outage does not remove it. Some courts have asked parties to help with mailings they would normally handle; that is on the court's own page if it applies.

If you are a creditor

You may learn of a case later than usual, because the notice that would have reached you by mail is delayed. Deadlines that run from the notice date are a question for your court, so watch the docket directly if you know a case exists. The National Creditor Registration Service, where creditors manage electronic noticing, is among the offline sites.

If you are an attorney or a trustee

Your NEF e-mails continue. What changes is the paper side: notices the BNC would have mailed are not going out, and several courts have published temporary instructions about who mails what, how to pull the creditor list from CM/ECF, and what to file if you cannot attach the court's matrix. Those instructions differ by district. The clerk's office is the right call for anything not covered on the court's page.

Nothing on this page tells you whom you must serve. That depends on your court, the document, and any orders in your case. The Open Bankruptcy Project is an independent 501(c)(3) public charity, not a court, not the BNC, and not its contractor. For your own case, talk to a lawyer, a legal aid office, or the court's pro se help desk if it has one.

Questions

Is my bankruptcy case still moving during the outage?

Yes, by every court statement we have read. Courts remain open, electronic filing continues, hearings proceed unless a court orders otherwise, and deadlines are not automatically extended. What is interrupted is the mailing of court-issued notices.

Do I have to mail notices myself now?

It depends on your court, the document, and any orders in your case. Courts have said the disruption does not change a party's existing duty to serve the documents it files. Some have posted temporary instructions. Check your court's website or call the clerk.

When will the BNC be back?

As of October 22, 2026, no restoration date had been announced. The U.S. Courts' October 15 statement said the contractor was working to restore operations. We will update the status line at the top of this page when the courts announce a change.

Was my information exposed?

The October 15 statement describes a cybersecurity incident affecting the contractor's systems; it does not say that debtor or creditor information was taken, and we do not know. If the courts publish more, we will link it here. Do not rely on this page, or on anyone's summary, for that question.

Is the Open Bankruptcy Project connected to the courts or the BNC?

No. We are an independent nonprofit that publishes research and free tools about the bankruptcy courts. This page summarizes public court statements and links to them.
