Editorial policy

How the pages on this site are written, reviewed, corrected, and funded, and the line we hold between information and advice.

Published 2026-09-20. Reviewed by Open Bankruptcy Project staff.
Home/About/Editorial policy

Who writes the pages

Pages are written and reviewed by Open Bankruptcy Project staff and volunteers and published under the organization's name. We do not attach individual bylines. Every guide cites the statute, rule, or official form it describes, and every count cites the source and date span it was computed from, so a reader can check the page against the primary record without taking anyone's word for it.

Guides are written from the primary sources: Title 11 of the United States Code, the Federal Rules of Bankruptcy Procedure, the Official Bankruptcy Forms, the U.S. Trustee Program's published figures, and the courts' own local rules. Data pages are built from the sources listed on the data methods page.

Review cadence

  • Each consumer guide carries a last reviewed date near its title. The date changes only when someone re-reads the page against its sources, not when a template or footer changes.
  • We re-check statutory citations, fee schedules, and dollar thresholds when the underlying figure changes. The U.S. Trustee median-income figures change every April 1 and November 1; the filing-fee schedule and the Bankruptcy Code's dollar amounts adjust on their own cycles.
  • Data pages are re-derived at each source refresh, and the refresh date is stated on the page.
  • Every guide is re-read at least once a year regardless of whether anything is known to have changed.

Corrections

If a page is wrong, we want to know. Send a sourced correction to info@openbankruptcyproject.org or use the contact form. Directory entries for trustees, clerks, and judges have their own accuracy and corrections process.

A confirmed error is fixed on the page. A material correction (a wrong figure, a wrong citation, a statement that reversed meaning) is noted on the page with the date it was corrected. We do not silently rewrite a figure that has already been cited elsewhere; the old value and the correction stay visible.

Independence and funding

  • We receive no referral fees. No link on this site pays us when you click it or when you hire anyone.
  • We have no attorney affiliation. The organization is not a law firm, does not employ attorneys to represent readers, and does not recommend, rank, or rate individual attorneys or firms.
  • We accept no paid placements and run no advertising on our pages.
  • Funding is donations from individual readers and a Google Ad Grant, an in-kind grant of search advertising that Google provides to eligible nonprofits. Google's grant pays for ads that point at our own free pages; it does not pay us money, and it does not influence what the pages say.
  • The organization is a 501(c)(3) public charity, EIN 41-5159631. Our IRS determination letter is linked from the about page.

What this site is not

This site is not legal advice and is not a substitute for a licensed attorney. Pages explain how the bankruptcy system works in general terms and point readers to free and low-cost help. When a reader writes to us with a question about their own case, we answer with general information and referrals, not with advice applied to their facts. Tools on this site compute what the statute or rule says on the numbers you enter; they do not recommend a chapter, a filing date, or a course of action.

The site is also not a directory of attorneys, not a lead-generation service, and not a place to look up a named individual for any purpose other than locating a public court record.
