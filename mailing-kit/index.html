Notice & Mailing Kit

Print bankruptcy notice packets, creditor labels, and mailing checklists for free. Your files stay in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, and no account is needed.

Why this exists

Bankruptcy runs on mailed paper. The court's own notices normally go out through the BNC. When that stops, the people who still have something to mail are left doing a repetitive job by hand: one copy of a notice for every creditor, a label or envelope for each, and some record of what went where. Thirty creditors and a two-page notice is sixty sheets, thirty envelopes, and a list. The job is not hard. It is tedious, and tedium is where mistakes happen.

This kit is a small utility in that line: it takes the repetition out of the mailing task without taking any of the decisions. It was built during the October 2026 disruption, and it stays useful after it, for anyone who has to mail a document to a list of parties.

Three things it does

Example 1
Print 30 court-notice packets
Open your notice PDF, paste 30 addresses, and get one exact copy per recipient, optionally with a separator sheet for sorting.
Example 2
Make creditor mailing labels
Avery 5160 / 8160 sheets, 30 per page, with an alignment test sheet and offsets for printers that drift. Or #10 envelopes, one per recipient.
Example 3
Keep a record of what you actually mailed
A checklist to tick as pieces go out, and an unsigned service-log worksheet you fill in afterward. Neither is a certificate of service.

Court-issued notices versus your own service duties

Two different things get mailed in a bankruptcy case, and the disruption affects only one of them.

  • Notices the court issues (the meeting-of-creditors notice, many orders) are normally mailed by the BNC on the court's behalf. During the disruption, courts have said those mailings may be delayed, and some courts have published temporary instructions for how parties can help.
  • Documents a party must serve itself (a motion, an application, an objection, anything your rules say the filer serves) were always the filing party's job. Courts that have spoken on this have said the disruption does not change that.

Attorneys and other registered CM/ECF users continue to receive Notices of Electronic Filing by e-mail. Several courts have also described how to pull the creditor list from CM/ECF, and have warned that the creditor list does not include every party in interest. If you are not sure which category your document falls into, the court clerk's office can tell you about the filing process, and a lawyer or legal aid office can tell you what applies to your case.

How it works

  1. Start with court guidance. Pick your district. Read what the court has said before you print anything.
  2. Choose outputs. Labels, #10 envelopes, packet copies, a checklist, a service-log worksheet, or any mix.
  3. Add recipients. Paste address blocks, open a CSV or text file from your computer, or type them. The review table flags blanks, possible duplicates, long lines, and missing ZIP codes. It never deletes a duplicate or changes an address for you.
  4. Add the document. Open the court-issued notice or other original PDF from your device. Its pages are copied as they are. If you have no original, you can still make labels and a checklist. The kit never invents a notice, a case number, a date, a mailing obligation, a certificate, or an address.
  5. Review and generate. See the counts (recipients, copies, sheets, label sheets, envelopes, pieces of postage), then download print-ready PDFs. Large jobs are split into several files so your browser stays responsive.
  6. Record what you mailed, afterward. Dates start blank. Export the log as a CSV or an unsigned worksheet. If your court requires a certificate of service, use the court's form or format and sign it yourself.

Privacy and limits

Files stay on this device

The workspace processes your recipient list and your PDF in browser memory. It makes no request that carries a name, an address, or a document, and it does not write to browser storage, cookies, or the address bar. The PDF library and CSV parser are bundled with the page, not loaded from a third party at run time. You can confirm this yourself in your browser's developer tools, Network tab. Reset or a page reload clears everything.

The workspace page loads our usual Google Analytics tag, with advertising features off, and records a single event when a generated file is downloaded. That event carries only which kinds of file were made (for example "labels" or "packets"), never counts, names, filenames, or anything from your documents. Details are in our privacy policy.

What it does not do

  • It does not decide whom you must serve, and it does not read your court's rules for you.
  • It does not verify addresses, buy postage, or mail anything. It does not file anything with any court.
  • It does not validate or alter a court-issued original, and it refuses password-protected PDFs.
  • It does not give legal advice. For your case, talk to a lawyer, a legal aid office, or the court's pro se help desk if it has one.

Questions

Does this tool tell me whom I have to serve?

No. Whom you must serve, and whether you must mail anything at all, depends on your court, the document, and any orders in your case. The kit prepares a packet only after you have determined that from your court's instructions.

Where do my files go?

Nowhere. They are processed in your browser's memory and are not uploaded, stored, or sent to us or anyone else. Closing the tab or pressing Reset clears them.

Is this a U.S. Courts or Bankruptcy Noticing Center service?

No. Open Bankruptcy Project is an independent 501(c)(3) public charity. This kit is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to the federal courts, the BNC, or its contractor.

Does generating the packet count as service?

No. Generating, downloading, or printing files does not serve anyone. The service log starts blank and records only what you enter after you actually mail the pieces, and it is not a certificate of service.

What if my court's requirements are unclear?

Call the clerk's office. They cannot give legal advice, but they can explain the filing process and point you to any temporary instructions. Several courts have posted FAQs during the disruption; your court's website is the first place to look.
