Official Bankruptcy Form 103B

Official Form 103B: Application to Have the Chapter 7 Filing Fee Waived

The form an individual files with a Chapter 7 petition to ask the court to waive the filing fee entirely.

Download Official Form 103B (PDF, uscourts.gov)

Current version: effective December 1, 2015 (form marked 12/15), from uscourts.gov. Official form page.

Your court may also require local forms. Check your bankruptcy court's website before filing.

What Form 103B is

Official Form 103B is an application asking the bankruptcy court to waive the Chapter 7 filing fee. The authority for the waiver is 28 U.S.C. Section 1930(f)(1), which lets the court waive the fee in a Chapter 7 case for an individual. Under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 1006(c), the clerk must accept an individual's voluntary Chapter 7 petition when it comes with a completed and signed Form 103B.

The Chapter 7 fee is $338: a $245 filing fee under Section 1930(a)(1)(A), plus the $78 administrative fee and the $15 trustee fee from the Judicial Conference's miscellaneous fee schedule.

Who qualifies

The official instructions say that for the fee to be waived, all of these statements must be true:

  • The case is filed under Chapter 7.
  • The filer is an individual.
  • The total combined monthly income for the filer's family is less than 150% of the official poverty guideline last published by the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services.
  • The filer cannot afford to pay the fee in installments.

Section 1930(f)(1) states the same two tests: income less than 150 percent of the poverty line for a family of that size, and inability to pay the fee in installments. The instructions point to uscourts.gov bankruptcy case policies for more on the guidelines. The form defines "family" as the filer, a spouse, and dependents, and the instructions note that family may differ from the "household" used on Schedules I and J.

A filer under Chapter 11, 12, or 13, or a filer who can pay in installments, would look at Official Form 103A for installment payments instead.

What the form asks

  • Part 1: family size, the family's average monthly net income, any non-cash government assistance, expected income changes, and why the fee cannot be paid in installments within 120 days.
  • Part 2: average monthly expenses and who else helps pay them.
  • Part 3: property, including cash, accounts, a home, other real estate, vehicles, and money owed to the filer. A filer who has already completed Schedule A/B attaches copies and skips to Part 4.
  • Part 4: payments made or promised to anyone for help with the bankruptcy, and any bankruptcy filed in the last 8 years.
  • Part 5: a signature under penalty of perjury declaring that the filer cannot afford the fee in full or in installments.

When it is filed and what attaches

The official instructions list Form 103B among the documents filed on the date the case is opened, for a Chapter 7 filer who meets the waiver criteria. Attachments the form and instructions mention:

  • Copies of Schedule A/B, if already completed (form, Part 3).
  • The bankruptcy petition preparer's Notice, Declaration, and Signature (Official Form 119), if a preparer helped.

What the court can decide

According to the instructions, after reviewing the application the court may waive the fee, set a hearing, or require the fee to be paid in installments or in full. The order printed at the end of the form shows three outcomes:

  • Granted. The order states that the court may still order the fee paid later if developments in the case show the waiver was unwarranted.
  • Denied. The court sets payment terms. The order states that the debtor may propose a different timetable, and may use Form 103A to do so.
  • Scheduled for hearing. The order states that if the debtor does not appear, the court may deny the application.

Common problems the form and statute point to

  • Wrong chapter. Section 1930(f)(1) and the form title limit this waiver to Chapter 7.
  • Income at or above the limit. The test is income less than 150% of the poverty guideline for the family's size.
  • No explanation on line 5. The form asks why the fee cannot be paid in installments within 120 days.
  • Missing the hearing. The order form states that the court may deny the application if the debtor does not appear.
  • An unsigned application. Rule 1006(c) requires a completed and signed application.

Frequently asked questions

Who qualifies for a Chapter 7 bankruptcy fee waiver?
Under 28 U.S.C. Section 1930(f)(1) and the official instructions, the filer must be an individual filing under Chapter 7, the family's total combined monthly income must be less than 150% of the official poverty guideline for the family's size, and the filer must be unable to pay the fee in installments.
Can Form 103B waive the Chapter 13 filing fee?
No. Form 103B and Section 1930(f)(1) cover the Chapter 7 filing fee. For other chapters, Official Form 103A is the application to pay the fee in installments.
What happens if the fee waiver is denied?
The order on Form 103B states that the court sets payment terms, and the debtor may propose a different timetable, for example on Official Form 103A.
