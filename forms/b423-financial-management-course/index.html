Official Bankruptcy Form 423 Abrogated

Official Form 423: Certification About a Financial Management Course (Abrogated)

Form 423 was discontinued effective December 1, 2024. Debtors now file the course provider's certificate of completion, unless the provider has already notified the court.

Download Official Form 423 Abrogation Notice (PDF, uscourts.gov)

Status: abrogated effective December 1, 2024, per uscourts.gov. There is no current version of Form 423 to fill out. Official form page.

Your court may also require local forms. Check your bankruptcy court's website before filing.

Form 423 is no longer used

The uscourts.gov forms library lists Official Form 423 as abrogated effective December 1, 2024. The official notice states that the form, which was used to document completion of a required course on personal financial management, was discontinued on that date.

What is filed instead

The notice explains that an individual debtor who has completed the course now files the certificate of course completion issued by the approved provider (often called a Certificate of Debtor Education), if the provider has not already notified the court. The rule behind this is Fed. R. Bankr. P. 1007(b)(7), which requires the provider's certificate unless:

  • an approved provider has notified the court that the debtor completed the course after filing the petition, or
  • the debtor is not required to complete a course as a condition of discharge.

Who the requirement covers

Rule 1007(b)(7) applies to an individual debtor in a Chapter 7 or Chapter 13 case, and in a Chapter 11 case in which Section 1141(d)(3) applies.

Deadlines under Rule 1007(c)(4)

Unless the court extends the time, the certificate is due:

  • Chapter 7: within 60 days after the first date set for the meeting of creditors under Section 341.
  • Chapter 11 or Chapter 13: no later than the date the last payment is made under the plan, or the date a motion for a discharge is filed under Section 1141(d)(5)(B) or Section 1328(b).

Why the course matters to the discharge

In Chapter 7, 11 U.S.C. Section 727(a)(11) lists failure to complete the personal financial management course after filing as a ground for denying the discharge. In Chapter 13, 11 U.S.C. Section 1328(g)(1) states that the court shall not grant a discharge unless the debtor completed the course after filing. Both sections have exceptions for a person described in Section 109(h)(4) and for districts where the U.S. trustee or bankruptcy administrator determines that approved courses are not adequate.

Common problems

  • Filing the old form. Form 423 was abrogated effective December 1, 2024. The rule now calls for the provider's certificate.
  • Taking the course before filing. Rule 1007(b)(7), Section 727(a)(11), and Section 1328(g)(1) all refer to a course completed after the petition is filed.
  • Missing the deadline. The Chapter 7 deadline runs from the first date set for the meeting of creditors, not from the date of the meeting that actually takes place.
  • Assuming the provider filed it. The exception applies only when an approved provider has notified the court.

Frequently asked questions

Is Official Form 423 still used?
No. uscourts.gov lists Official Form 423 as abrogated effective December 1, 2024.
What is filed instead of Form 423?
Under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 1007(b)(7), the debtor files the certificate of course completion issued by the approved provider, unless the provider has notified the court that the debtor completed the course after filing, or the debtor is not required to complete one.
When is the financial management course certificate due?
Under Rule 1007(c)(4), in Chapter 7 it is due within 60 days after the first date set for the meeting of creditors. In Chapter 11 or 13 it is due no later than the date of the last plan payment or the date a motion for a discharge is filed under Section 1141(d)(5)(B) or 1328(b). The court may extend the time.
