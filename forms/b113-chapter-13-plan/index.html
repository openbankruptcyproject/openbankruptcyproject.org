Official Bankruptcy Form 113 Chapter 13

Official Form 113: Chapter 13 Plan

The national form for a Chapter 13 repayment plan. A district may instead require its own single local plan form under Rule 3015.1.

Download Official Form 113 (PDF, uscourts.gov)

Current version: effective December 1, 2017 (form marked 12/17), from uscourts.gov. Official form page.

Your court may also require local forms. Check your bankruptcy court's website before filing.

Check for a local plan form first. Under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 3015(c)(1), a Chapter 13 debtor must use Form 113 unless the court has adopted a local form under Rule 3015.1. Rule 3015.1 allows a district to require a single local form instead of Form 113. The bankruptcy court's website shows whether a local plan form is in use.

What Form 113 is

Official Form 113 is the plan a Chapter 13 debtor proposes for paying creditors over time. The committee note describes it as the required plan form in all Chapter 13 cases, except where Rule 3015(c) permits a local form. The form itself warns debtors that an option appearing on the form does not mean it is appropriate in a given case or permitted in a given district, and that plans that do not comply with local rules and judicial rulings may not be confirmable.

What a local plan form must contain

Rule 3015.1 sets requirements for any local form used in place of Form 113. Among them, the local form must:

  • be adopted for the district after public notice and an opportunity for comment;
  • number and label each paragraph in boldface with a heading;
  • open with a paragraph where the debtor indicates whether the plan contains a nonstandard provision, limits a secured claim based on collateral value, or avoids a security interest or lien;
  • contain separate paragraphs on curing and maintaining home loan payments, domestic support obligations, claims described in the final paragraph of Section 1325(a), and surrender of property; and
  • end with a place for nonstandard provisions and a certification by the debtor's attorney, or by an unrepresented debtor, that there are no other nonstandard provisions.

When the plan is due

  • Under Rule 3015(b)(1), the plan is filed with the petition or within 14 days after the petition is filed. The time may not be extended except for cause and on notice as the court orders.
  • Under Rule 3015(b)(2), if a case is converted to Chapter 13, the plan is due within 14 days after conversion.

How Form 113 is organized

Part 1: Notices

Warnings to debtors and creditors, and three check boxes stating whether the plan limits a secured claim, avoids a lien or security interest, or includes nonstandard provisions.

Part 2: Plan Payments and Length

Regular payments to the trustee, how they are made, and treatment of income tax refunds.

Parts 3 to 5: Claims

Secured claims (Part 3), fees and priority claims (Part 4), and nonpriority unsecured claims (Part 5).

Parts 6 to 9

Executory contracts and unexpired leases (Part 6), vesting of estate property (Part 7), nonstandard plan provisions (Part 8), and signatures (Part 9).

Nonstandard provisions

Rule 3015(c)(2) defines a nonstandard provision as one that is not included in the form or deviates from it. With either Form 113 or a local form, a nonstandard provision is effective only if it is placed in the section designated for nonstandard provisions. Part 1 of Form 113 adds that if an item is checked "Not Included," or if both boxes are checked, the provision will be ineffective if set out later in the plan.

Service and objections

  • Under Rule 3015(d), if the plan was not included with the notice of the confirmation hearing, the debtor must serve the plan on the trustee and creditors when it is filed.
  • Under Rule 3015(f)(1), unless the court orders otherwise, an objection to confirmation must be filed and served at least 7 days before the confirmation hearing.

Common problems the rules and form point to

  • Using Form 113 where a local form is required. Rule 3015(c)(1) makes Form 113 the default only when the court has not adopted a local form.
  • A nonstandard provision in the wrong place. Outside Part 8, or without the matching Part 1 box, it is not effective.
  • A late plan. The 14-day period in Rule 3015(b) is extended only for cause and on notice.
  • An unserved plan. Rule 3015(d) requires service when the plan did not go out with the confirmation-hearing notice.
  • An altered form. Rule 9009(a) requires Official Forms to be used without alteration except as the rules, the form, or the national instructions allow.

Frequently asked questions

Do all Chapter 13 debtors use Official Form 113?
Not in every district. Under Fed. R. Bankr. P. 3015(c)(1), the debtor must use Form 113 unless the court has adopted a local form under Rule 3015.1, which allows a district to require a single local plan form instead.
When must a Chapter 13 plan be filed?
Under Rule 3015(b)(1), with the petition or within 14 days after the petition is filed. In a case converted to Chapter 13, within 14 days after conversion.
What is a nonstandard provision in a Chapter 13 plan?
Rule 3015(c)(2) defines it as a provision not included in the form or that deviates from it. It is effective only if placed in the section of the form designated for nonstandard provisions.
